Information sheet for participating clinicians

Expert evaluation of patient-level phenotypic profiles extracted automatically from published case reports.

Study team: [UNIVERSITY / LAB NAME] Principal investigator: [PI NAME, TITLE] Ethics approval: [ETHICS COMMITTEE, APPROVAL NO.]

1. Why are we doing this study?

Rare-disease research needs precise descriptions of patients' clinical features. Published case reports contain this information, but extracting it by hand is slow. We have built a computer pipeline, based on large language models, that reads a case report, finds the abnormal phenotypic features of each patient described, and codes them with terms from the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO).

Before such profiles can be used, we need to know how accurate they are. Clinical judgement is the reference we need: this study asks clinicians to check the pipeline's output.

2. Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because you are a clinician with experience in describing patient phenotypes [e.g. clinical genetics, paediatrics, neurology]. We aim to include [NUMBER] clinicians.

3. What will I be asked to do?

  • Sign in to the PhenoEval web application with your name and email address.
  • Review 50 published case reports. Every participant receives the same set of articles.
  • For each phenotype highlighted in an article, decide whether its HPO term fits (accept, revise or reject it), check that it is assigned to the right patient, and add any phenotypes the pipeline missed.
  • Submit your answers for each article. You can work at your own pace, stop at any time and come back later: every answer is saved as you go.

We estimate the whole task takes about [ESTIMATED HOURS] hours in total. Please work independently and do not discuss the articles with other participants until the study is over, so that we can compare answers between clinicians.

The highlights and terms you see were produced automatically and may be wrong; that is what you are asked to judge. You will not see the reference annotations the study uses for comparison.

4. Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is entirely voluntary. If you agree, you can still withdraw at any time, without giving a reason and without any consequence for you. How to withdraw, and what happens to your answers, is explained in section 8.

5. Are there any risks or benefits?

There are no known risks beyond the time the task takes. The articles are published case reports: no identifiable patient data is shown or collected. There is no direct benefit to you, but your work will help measure how reliable automatically extracted phenotype profiles are, and so improve tools for rare-disease research.
