How to perform the task

For each patient described in the article, the mentions of abnormal clinical phenotypes associated with the patient are highlighted in the article text (left panel, Figure 1). Each mention was automatically assigned a term from the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO), or flagged when no fitting term was found. Click a mention to open its decision card (right panel, Figure 1) and verify that 1 the predicted HPO term fits the phenotypic abnormality stated in the text (for a mention without a term, confirm that no fitting HPO term exists, or assign one if you find it). Reject mentions that are not an observed phenotypic abnormality of a patient. 2 For a genuine phenotype mention, verify that it is assigned to the correct patient. If not, re-assign it. 3 If you find other mentions of abnormal phenotypes that are not highlighted for this patient, add them manually.

1How to validate the HPO term of a mention

For every phenotype mention (a span in the text), Accept, Revise or Reject its HPO term.

Accept · The HPO term fits

Accept the HPO term if it fits the phenotype mention.

“echocardiography revealed coarctation of the aorta✓” → Coarctation of aorta HP:0001680

Revise · Find a fitting HPO term

Revise the predicted HPO term if you think the current term does not accurately capture the stated phenotypic abnormality, and a better fit exists in the HPO. You can revise it in two ways:

  1. 1Select a different term among the candidates provided for this mention. Switch to Lineage to see how they relate in the hierarchy.
  2. 2If none of the candidates fits, click Search HPO to search the full ontology by name, synonym or ID, and choose the best match. Any term in the lineage tree can be chosen.

Click Confirm to save your revision. If neither gives a term that describes the phenotype without changing its meaning, click No fitting HPO term in the search: the mention is recorded as No code (an ontology gap).

“eversion of the lateral third of the lower eyelids” predicted as Abnormal eyelid morphology: correct but too broad → Eversion of lateral third of lower eyelids HP:0007655

Reject · Not a patient phenotype

Use Reject only if the highlighted mention does not describe an observed phenotypic abnormality of a patient. This includes the following cases:

  1. 1The text span does not describe a clinical phenotype.
  2. 2The text span describes a normal, absent or not confirmed clinical phenotype.
  3. 3The abnormal phenotype does not belong to a patient from this study (e.g. a general mention of a clinical abnormality).

“Her hearing was normal✕” predicted as Hearing impairment → Reject (a normal finding)

Mentions without a predicted HPO term · No code

For some mentions the pipeline found no HPO term that fits. They are highlighted in the text like the other mentions to review, and their card shows No HPO term predicted. Choose one of:

  1. 1Confirm · No fitting HPO term: you agree that no HPO term describes the phenotype. The mention is recorded as No code (an ontology gap).
  2. 2Revise · Find a fitting HPO term: a term does exist. Select it among the candidates or search the HPO, as for any revision.
  3. 3Reject · Not a patient phenotype: the same cases as above.

2How to validate the patient assignment

For each phenotype mention with a validated HPO term, verify that the phenotype is assigned to the correct patient.

Wrong patient?

Click Wrong patient? to reassign the phenotype mention: choose the correct patient from the list. Use Someone else and enter the identifier of the correct patient from the article if it is not in the list.

3How to add a missing phenotype

If you identify an abnormal phenotypic feature of this patient that is not highlighted in the text, add it as follows.

Add a missed phenotype

  1. Click Add a missed phenotype in the right-hand panel, below the phenotype mentions.
  2. Select the words of the phenotype in the article text, within one paragraph or one table cell, and click Use selection. The span cannot be typed, so that its exact place in the article is recorded.
  3. Search the fitting HPO term by name, synonym or ID, and click Confirm.

The phenotype will be highlighted for the correct patient. You can Revise or Remove it at any time.

The phenotypic profile

You can see the summary of the patient’s phenotypic features you reviewed in the phenotypic profile (right-hand panel): the phenotypes you accepted, revised or added, shown by their HPO term and grouped by organ system, and those you confirmed as No code, shown by the text of the mention in a group of their own. Mentions still to review and rejected mentions are not shown. When a phenotype has several mentions in the text, their number is shown next to it (×2). Click a phenotype to open its mention.

Good to know

  • Use Revise, not Reject, if the predicted HPO term doesn’t fit the phenotype mention.
  • When revising an HPO term, prefer the most specific term the text supports. Code the mention.
  • Every click is saved. You can leave and come back at any time.
